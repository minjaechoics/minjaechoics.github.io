I am MinJae Choi, an undergraduate at [KAIST](https://kaist.ac.kr) majoring in Computer Science and AI Systems, with a minor in Mathematics. My research interests lie in **Efficient ML**, especially for AI foundation models — end-to-end foundation model systems spanning architecture design, large-scale training, low-bit quantization, efficient deployment, and inference kernel optimization. I'm also interested in theoretical computer science, particularly algorithmic graph structure theory and algorithm design for NP-hard problems.

I am an undergraduate researcher at the [Flexible Machine Learning Lab](https://flexml.kaist.ac.kr/), advised by Prof. Insu Han and co-advised by Prof. Sukmin Yun (Hanyang Univ. ERICA), working on near-1-bit quantization for trillion-scale foundation models in collaboration with Samsung Electronics' Device Solution Division. I am also active in [Include](https://kaist-include.io/), KAIST's AI club, where I have organized and taught seminars on generative models, graph AI, and computer vision.

Outside of the lab, I compete in AI/ML competitions and hackathons: [ZipJiGi](https://github.com/minjaechoics/ZipJiGi) won an Excellence Award at the 2026 KB Financial AI Challenge, my team advanced to the finals of the LG AI Research EXAONE Compression Competition, and I've worked on LoRA adapters for the NVIDIA Nemotron3 Reasoning Challenge and on [Amplify](/amplify.html) for the Microsoft Imagine Cup. I also bring an Industrial Design background to my engineering work — see the [design portfolio](/design.html) for that side of things.

A public [wiki](/wiki.html) of my course notes from KAIST (CS / EE / ID) is also available, along with a small [competition-tracking dashboard](/falcon.html) I built for fun.
